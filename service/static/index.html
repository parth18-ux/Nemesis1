RuseTect · scam and mule detection · synthetic data

Catch the ring.
Clear the rest.

A layered AI filter for scam lures, mule accounts and scam money flows. A small gatekeeper clears most payments; the rest are scored by gradient boosted trees, checked against consented message context and the account graph, and sent to an analyst with reasons. The model never blocks on its own.

What the model sees

A single transfer looks normal. The pattern around it does not.

  1. 01

    History, not just the payment

    22 features per payment, each computed only from earlier activity: money in then straight out, sudden fan out, money flowing back.

  2. 02

    Reasons an analyst can use

    Every alert lists its top drivers in plain words, such as "minutes since money came in: 0" or "new counterparty".

  3. 03

    Rings by typology

    Offline, against IBM's labelled synthetic rings: fan in 90%, cycles 88%, gather scatter 87%, fan out 86% caught. Bipartite rings are hardest at 52%.

Why this model

Boosted trees, because of the data

Each payment becomes 31 numbers about the accounts' past behaviour. Many are missing for new accounts, and laundering is under 0.2% of payments. Gradient boosted trees fit that shape.

Features matter more than the algorithm

XGBoost scores 0.172 PR AUC on raw payment fields and 0.715 with account history features. The top four tree models sit between 0.699 and 0.715.

L1 is small on purpose

30 shallow trees approve about 80% of payments in about 0.3 ms while keeping about 99% of laundering for L2.

L2 averages XGBoost and LightGBM

Two boosters grown differently give a steadier score: 0.716 PR AUC for the live average against 0.722 for its XGBoost half, so the case is robustness, not a higher number.
